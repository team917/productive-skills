# Strategy Frameworks

Operate like a chief of staff who names the real challenge before proposing a plan.
GOOD STRATEGY KERNEL (Rumelt): Every real strategy has three parts - a diagnosis that names the actual challenge, a guiding policy for the approach, and coherent action where every move reinforces the others. Skip the diagnosis and what's left is "bad strategy": goals dressed up as a plan.
PLAYING TO WIN: Strategy is five reinforcing choices - winning aspiration, where to play, how to win, core capabilities, management systems. If the strategy could apply to any company in the industry, it's a horoscope, not a strategy. A plan is just a to-do list with a mission statement stapled on top.
OKR CALIBRATION: Set each Key Result at roughly 5-out-of-10 confidence at quarter start - a genuine coin flip. If it doesn't produce a knot in your stomach it's too easy. Landing near 70% is the target; hitting 100% means it was sandbagged, hitting 20% means the team overreached.
OKR VS HEALTH METRICS: OKRs get pushed toward; health metrics (revenue, satisfaction, system stability, team wellbeing) get protected. A red health metric triggers a "code red" that PAUSES OKR work until it's fixed - growth never gets prioritized over a broken foundation.
OKR ANTI-PATTERNS: Never cascade by making your manager's Key Result your Objective, and never write individual-level OKRs - keep them team-level or they collapse into disguised performance reviews and kill collaboration. Do less, better: one real Objective at a time, not five diluted ones.
7 POWERS: Durable competitive advantage requires BOTH a benefit and a barrier - a benefit rivals can freely copy is not Power. Value scales as market size times power. Map the seven power types to company stage: origination favors counter-positioning and cornered resources, takeoff favors scale/network economies and switching costs, stability favors process power and branding.
V2MOM (Benioff): Vision, Values, Methods, Obstacles, Measures - in that exact order. Obstacles is the most-skipped, most load-bearing element; the sequence cascades from individual to team to company so everyone's V2MOM ladders up.
WORKING BACKWARDS (Amazon): Start every initiative from the customer with a PR/FAQ, decide via a 6-page narrative memo read silently at the start of the meeting (never slides), assign one Single-Threaded Leader per initiative, and manage to INPUT metrics (actions a team controls) rather than output metrics they can only hope for.
POSITIONING (Dunford): Five components in sequence - competitive alternatives, unique attributes, value, target segment, market category - feed directly into the sales pitch. The pitch should sell the point of view before it sells the product.
PRE-MORTEM: Before committing to a plan, imagine it has already failed and work backward through the failure categories to find the real risks. Apply the "monkeys and pedestals" test - build the hardest, most-likely-to-kill-it part of a project first, not the easy, safe part.
