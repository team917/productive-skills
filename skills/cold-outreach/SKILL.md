# Cold Outreach

Operate like an SDR who treats every sequence as a measured experiment, not a guess.
HOOK: Rank hooks by reply rate - timeline (deadline/event) beats numbers, numbers beat social proof, social proof beats a plain problem statement, roughly 3-4x meeting-rate spread top to bottom. Default to a timeline hook whenever a real deadline or event exists.
LENGTH: Reply rate falls almost linearly with length - 50-75 words converts near 12%, 75-125 near 10%, 125-200 near 7%, past 200 words it collapses to ~2%. Hard cap outbound at 125 words, never past 150.
CADENCE: Four emails on a 0/3/10/17-day schedule, each carrying a DISTINCT angle (pain, then value, then social proof, then a breakup) - a bump with no new information reads as pressure, not persistence. Most replies land before day 10; a fifth-plus follow-up generates more unsubscribes than replies.
SEQUENCE MATH: A single email has the best per-email reply rate, but a full 4-5 email sequence captures roughly 30% more total replies than stopping after one - both facts are true at once, so always sequence, but keep each email good enough to stand alone.
SUBJECT LINES: 3-5 words, lowercase, never the company name (reads as mass-send). Real personalization tied to something specific about the prospect lifts opens and replies meaningfully; generic "salesy" phrasing costs opens. Ship 3 variants (question / observation / result) and don't call a winner before ~500 sends.
TARGETING: Depth beats breadth - reaching 1-2 well-chosen contacts per account outperforms blanketing 10+ contacts at the same account by roughly 2x on reply rate. A tight, well-researched list of 50 beats a loose list of 500.
MULTICHANNEL: Email plus LinkedIn plus phone roughly doubles or triples reply rate versus email-only - keep any single channel under half of total touchpoints in a sequence.
TIMING: Tuesday-Thursday, mid-morning in the prospect's timezone; Thursday is consistently the strongest single day. Never send first-thing Monday (inbox overflow) or Friday afternoon.
FRAMEWORK: PAS (Problem - Agitate - Solution) is the default body structure; switch to AIDA when there's a strong trigger event, BAB (Before/After/Bridge) when the contrast is quantifiable. One CTA per email - never drop a calendar link in email one, ask a low-friction question instead.
BENCHMARKS: 8-12% reply / 2-4% meeting rate is a realistic healthy B2B campaign; 15%+ reply puts a campaign in the top 5%. Use these to know whether a sequence actually underperformed before rewriting it.
